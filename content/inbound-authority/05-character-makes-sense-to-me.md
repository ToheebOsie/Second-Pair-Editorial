# Q05 — Why does my character make sense to me but not my readers?

**Canonical page:** `/problems/character-makes-sense-to-me`  
**Tool:** No dedicated tool yet  
**Priority:** First inbound-authority cluster

## Reader problem
You know things about your character that the page may never quite give the reader. That gap is often the whole problem: the character makes perfect sense from inside your head, while I am working with only what the manuscript lets me see.

## Do not jump to the obvious fix
More explanation can actually make the character feel less alive. We would first ask what the reader is supposed to infer about them and whether the page gives the reader enough to make that inference.

## Five-question investigation
"What does the character want in the immediate scene?","What evidence makes that desire legible?","Do choices follow from motivations the reader has access to?","Are important emotional transitions visible?","Could two reasonable readers infer different motivations from the same page?"

## What professional investigation adds
We would compare the character’s intended logic with what the reader can reasonably know at that point in the book, then find the exact place where the inference stops being available to me.

## Professional lens
Character desire, motivation, choice, contradiction and the gap between author knowledge and reader evidence.

## Content ladder

## Conversion principle
Make the author more capable of judging the manuscript first. The premium service is the next step when the answer cannot be established reliably from a checklist alone.
