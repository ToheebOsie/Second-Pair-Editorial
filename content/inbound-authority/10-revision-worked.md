# Q10 — How do I know whether my revision actually fixed the problem?

**Canonical page:** `/problems/revision-worked`  
**Tool:** No dedicated tool yet  
**Priority:** First inbound-authority cluster

## Reader problem
A revision can feel better and still leave the original problem in place. The useful comparison is between what you wanted to change and what actually changed for the reader.

## Do not jump to the obvious fix
Your own reaction matters. It just cannot answer the whole question. If the goal was to change the reading experience, we need to see whether that actually happened.

## Five-question investigation
"What was the original concern?","What change were you trying to produce?","What changed for the reader?","Did the revision introduce a new issue?","What evidence would justify considering the problem resolved?"

## What professional investigation adds
We would read the before and after with the original problem in mind: what changed, what stayed stubbornly the same, and whether the fix created a new problem somewhere else.

## Professional lens
Comparative rereading, original diagnosis, observable change, side effects and revision verification.

## Content ladder
1. Problem page answers the question.

## Conversion principle
Make the author more capable of judging the manuscript first. The premium service is the next step when the answer cannot be established reliably from a checklist alone.
