# V5.12 — First-12 Final Editorial & Voice Audit

## Verdict

**PASS with remediation completed.** V5.10/V5.11 had sound architecture but over-templated prose. V5.12 removes the repeated newsletter/long-form skeleton and replaces it with problem-specific reasoning.

## Audit criteria

- Intelligent, calm, direct, experienced, non-performative voice
- Professional rather than academic
- Human rather than corporate
- Observation separated from interpretation
- Symptom separated from cause
- No certainty theatre or universal craft rules
- Author agency preserved
- Content demonstrates diagnostic orientation before selling a service
- Problem-led acquisition remains intact

## Findings fixed

### 1. Repetitive newsletter architecture — FIXED
The prior issues repeatedly used the same “misdiagnose / reader-side test / what not to change / outside read” wording. Each newsletter now has a distinct diagnostic centre appropriate to its problem.

### 2. Repetitive long-form architecture — FIXED
The prior long-form drafts reused almost identical headings and paragraphs. V5.12 retains the underlying method but varies the reasoning and concrete test for each problem.

### 3. Generic social derivatives — FIXED
Social material now has ten different editorial functions per problem rather than sentence permutations.

### 4. Premium positioning — PRESERVED
The content still demonstrates the established proposition: evidence + interpretation + prioritization + decision support, rather than volume of comments or generic professionalism.

### 5. Acquisition model — PRESERVED
No cold outreach, unsolicited critique, scraping, marketplace bidding, or generic sales CTA has been introduced. The intended path remains problem discovery → useful answer → demonstration → trust → private inquiry.

## Corpus/brand-voice check

The accessible strategic corpus describes the desired public voice as intelligent, calm, evidence-oriented, direct, experienced, non-performative, respectful, confident without certainty theatre, specific, professional rather than academic, and human rather than corporate. The revised copy was tested against those criteria.

The supplied positioning also explicitly requires “Here's what I'm seeing,” evidence-led reader reconstruction, diagnosis before prescription, and protection of author agency. The revised copy follows those principles.

## Competitive check

The current market already sells editorial assessments and developmental editing as holistic feedback, often with per-word pricing. Reedsy currently describes editorial assessment as a detailed report on plot, structure and characterization and reports an average assessment around $1,500 for an 80,000-word manuscript. The content therefore should not compete by claiming merely to provide “professional feedback.” It must keep demonstrating the narrower diagnostic distinction.

## Final decision

**The first 12 are now suitable as the benchmark cluster for scaling.** Do not scale the remaining 88 by copying these sentences. Scale the *editorial method*: each new question must have its own mechanism, evidence test, likely misdiagnosis, reader-side language, and professional threshold.
