# Q03 — Why do beta readers disagree?

**Canonical page:** `/problems/beta-readers-disagree`  
**Tool:** /tools/feedback-conflict  
**Priority:** First inbound-authority cluster

## Reader problem
Readers can disagree for perfectly ordinary reasons: taste, expectations, genre habits, or simply different reactions to the same page. We would be less interested in counting votes than in finding out what the disagreement is actually about.

## Do not jump to the obvious fix
We would not decide by majority vote. One reader can notice something important that others miss, while five readers can share the same expectation that the manuscript is not trying to meet.

## Five-question investigation
"Are readers disagreeing about preference or observable events?","Do multiple readers describe the same underlying problem differently?","Are their expectations compatible with the intended audience?","Does the disagreement cluster around a specific passage or recur?","What decision are you actually trying to make from the feedback?"

## What professional investigation adds
We would sort the comments into what people agree on, where they genuinely disagree, what may simply be taste, and what can only be settled by returning to the manuscript.

## Professional lens
Preference versus observable evidence, audience expectation, recurring patterns and returning to the manuscript.

## Content ladder
1. Problem page answers the question.
2. Newsletter gives one useful distinction and one manuscript test.
3. Long-form article shows how the symptom can have different causes.
4. Social content extracts one sharp observation.
5. Tool provides a self-check where appropriate.
6. Service appears only when the remaining question requires manuscript-level investigation.

## Conversion principle
Make the author more capable of judging the manuscript first. The premium service is the next step when the answer cannot be established reliably from a checklist alone.
