# Q02 — Why do readers understand my plot but not care?

**Canonical page:** `/problems/readers-understand-but-dont-care`  
**Tool:** /tools/reader-friction  
**Priority:** First inbound-authority cluster

## Reader problem
A reader can understand exactly what is happening and still not feel much about it. Usually the question is not whether the plot is clear, but whether the reader has a reason to care what happens next.

## Do not jump to the obvious fix
Adding more emotion is not necessarily the fix. We would first ask what the story wants the reader to care about, and whether the page gives that thing enough consequence, attachment, or uncertainty to matter.

## Five-question investigation
"What does the reader want the character to achieve or avoid?","What consequence feels immediate rather than abstract?","What changes if the character fails?","Where does the reader form an expectation about the character?","Does the manuscript give that expectation a reason to matter?"

## What professional investigation adds
We would trace the feeling back to character desire, consequence, expectation, and what actually happens in the scene. “I did not care” is useful information, but it is a starting point rather than a diagnosis.

## Professional lens
Emotional involvement, character desire, consequence, expectation and the distinction between comprehension and investment.

## Content ladder
1. Problem page answers the question.
2. Newsletter gives one useful distinction and one manuscript test.
3. Long-form article shows how the symptom can have different causes.
4. Social content extracts one sharp observation.
5. Tool provides a self-check where appropriate.
6. Service appears only when the remaining question requires manuscript-level investigation.

## Conversion principle
Make the author more capable of judging the manuscript first. The premium service is the next step when the answer cannot be established reliably from a checklist alone.
