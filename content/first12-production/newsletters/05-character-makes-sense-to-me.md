# You know why your character did it. Does the reader?

**Second Pair Newsletter**  
**Canonical problem:** `/problems/character-makes-sense-to-me`

An author once knows much more about a character than a reader can possibly know. You may understand why she refuses to go home because you know what happened years ago. But if the manuscript has not given the reader that history—or a clue that points towards it—the refusal can look arbitrary.

Go back to the decision that caused the comment. What does the character want? What do they believe will happen? What has the reader seen that makes this choice understandable, even if they disagree with it?

The answer is not always a block of backstory. A small reaction earlier in the chapter may be enough. Sometimes the motive is already present, but it arrives after the decision and therefore cannot help the reader understand it in the moment.

**Try this:** Read the scene as though you know nothing beyond the pages that come before it. What can you infer about the character's reason?
