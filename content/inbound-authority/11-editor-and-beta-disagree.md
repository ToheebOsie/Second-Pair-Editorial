# Q11 — What should I do when my editor and beta readers disagree?

**Canonical page:** `/problems/editor-and-beta-disagree`  
**Tool:** /tools/feedback-conflict  
**Priority:** First inbound-authority cluster

## Reader problem
An editor and a beta reader may be talking about the same passage from completely different angles. One may be naming a craft problem; the other may simply be telling you what happened for them as a reader.

## Do not jump to the obvious fix
We would not solve the disagreement by deciding who has the bigger title. First find out whether they are even making the same kind of claim.

## Five-question investigation
"Are the two people actually answering the same question?","Is one response preference while the other identifies a craft mechanism?","Do other readers corroborate either response?","What manuscript evidence supports the concern?","What decision are you trying to make?"

## What professional investigation adds
We would put the comments side by side, translate what each person is actually saying, and then return to the pages. The goal is not to pick a winner; it is to find the useful question underneath the disagreement.

## Professional lens
Conflict classification, professional scope, reader preference, intended audience and manuscript evidence.

## Content ladder
1. Problem page answers the question.
2. Newsletter gives one useful distinction and one manuscript test.
3. Long-form article shows how the symptom can have different causes.
4. Social content extracts one sharp observation.
5. Tool provides a self-check where appropriate.
6. Service appears only when the remaining question requires manuscript-level investigation.

## Conversion principle
Make the author more capable of judging the manuscript first. The premium service is the next step when the answer cannot be established reliably from a checklist alone.
