# Your world may be clear to you and exhausting to a new reader

**Second Pair Newsletter**  
**Canonical problem:** `/problems/worldbuilding-too-much`

You know the old war, the family titles, the rules of the magic system and why the city is divided into seven districts. The reader is trying to understand the scene they are in.

A detail earns its place when it helps the reader picture something, understand a choice, recognise a danger or see why a consequence matters. If the chapter stops so the narrator can explain a treaty that no one uses in the scene, the information may be arriving too early.

This does not mean the world should be shallow. It means the reader can learn it in the order the story makes it useful.

**Try this:** Mark each paragraph of world explanation in one chapter. Beside it, write what the reader needs that information for at that moment. If you cannot find a job for it yet, see whether it can wait until a character acts on it.
