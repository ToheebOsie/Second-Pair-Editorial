# Second Pair Editorial — Client Acquisition Operating Playbook

This is a lightweight operating layer for the existing problem-led website. It is deliberately manual until there is evidence that a CRM or analytics platform is needed.

## Lead stages

1. **New enquiry** — received; no scope recommendation yet.
2. **Qualified** — genre, stage, concern, word count and desired decision fit the service.
3. **Sample under review** — if applicable; track time spent.
4. **Recommendation / quote sent** — route, deliverables, exclusions, price and timeline explained.
5. **Considering** — author is deciding or has raised a genuine question.
6. **Won** — price and scope agreed, payment terms confirmed.
7. **Deferred / lost / not a fit** — record only the reason the author actually gives or a neutral operational reason.
8. **In progress / complete** — record delivery and feedback.
9. **Repeat / referral** — record only when it happens.

## Reply standard

- State the concern in the author's own terms before recommending a service.
- Ask only for missing details that could change scope or recommendation.
- Recommend the smallest useful route supported by the information.
- State deliverables, exclusions, price basis, timeline and payment terms before work begins.
- Never promise publication, sales, a guaranteed outcome, or a diagnosis from unseen pages.
- Follow up once when appropriate; do not manufacture urgency or send repeated pressure messages.

## Monthly review

Record a baseline before setting targets. Review:

- enquiries by author-reported source and captured landing page;
- qualified enquiries by source and concern;
- sample requests, minutes spent and sample-to-paid conversion;
- quotes sent, paid projects and reasons for deferral/loss;
- average project value and time to payment;
- delivery time, satisfaction, repeat work and referrals;
- which manuscript concerns most often lead to a good service fit.

Use the captured attribution fields in enquiry emails with the manual pipeline. The website does not currently know whether a lead becomes qualified or pays, so those outcomes must be recorded manually.

## Privacy and learning

Do not paste manuscript text, unpublished plot details, or private author correspondence into the marketing tracker. Record anonymised patterns only. Request permission before publishing testimonials or case studies. Do not invent reasons when a lead goes quiet.

## Suggested first 30 days

1. Use the new intake attribution fields and pipeline template for every enquiry.
2. Track time spent preparing samples.
3. At month-end, review source, concern, stage, quote and outcome together.
4. Change one part of the acquisition or conversion process at a time, based on observed bottlenecks—not traffic alone.
