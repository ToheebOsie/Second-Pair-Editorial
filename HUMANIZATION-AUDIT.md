# Website Humanization Pass — Historical V5.4 and V5.21.3 follow-up

## Purpose

This pass removes polished, generic language from the public website and replaces it with plain professional wording that an author would expect to hear from a real editor.

## Approach used

The wording pass follows the humanization guidance in the supplied manuscript-development pipeline, especially the principles of:

- plain-language cleanup
- removing unnecessary abstraction
- avoiding repeated rhetorical frames
- varying sentence shape and paragraph rhythm
- keeping useful professional judgment without turning every sentence into a polished thesis
- separating what the reader noticed from what the editor thinks it may mean
- avoiding fake casualness, forced slang, deliberate mistakes and manufactured personality
- preserving the site's actual services, prices, claims and boundaries

## Main changes

- Replaced phrases such as `reader-centred editorial support`, `evidence-based`, `actionable`, `editorial journey`, `methodology`, `smallest sufficient service`, and similar process-heavy language in visible customer-facing copy.
- Reduced repeated use of `reader experience` where ordinary wording such as `how the story reads`, `how it feels to a reader`, or `what a reader notices` says the same thing more naturally.
- Reworked headings to sound like normal conversation between an author and an editor.
- Replaced abstract claims with concrete examples: getting lost, losing interest, wanting more information, a character choice not landing, pacing slowing down, or a revision creating a new problem.
- Removed overly polished contrast formulas and repeated `X → Y → Z` explanations from customer-facing prose.
- Kept service names and SEO terms where they are useful for search or clarity.
- Kept the site's commercial boundaries intact: no ghostwriting, no full-manuscript rewriting, transparent published rates, sample read, and author ownership of creative decisions.
