# You made the changes. Did the reading problem go away?

**Second Pair Newsletter**  
**Canonical problem:** `/problems/revision-worked`

A revised scene can look much better to its author because the author knows what has been added and why. That does not always mean the reader will notice the improvement.

Before you compare drafts, write down the original problem and what you changed to address it. Then reread the old and new versions at the point where the trouble used to happen.

Can the reader now follow the character's choice? Is the missing information available earlier? Has the cut removed a repetition, or has it taken away a detail needed later? A revision can solve one problem and create another.

Do not ask only whether the new draft is better. Ask someone unfamiliar with the old version what they understand at the point that used to cause trouble. Their account can show whether the change is doing its job.
