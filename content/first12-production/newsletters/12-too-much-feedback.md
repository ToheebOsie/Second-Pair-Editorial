# What should I do when I have too much manuscript feedback?

**Second Pair Newsletter**  
**Canonical problem:** `/problems/too-much-feedback`

## The useful distinction

The problem with a large pile of notes is not the number of comments. It is that comments can sit at different levels: sentence preference, scene problem, character issue, structural consequence, or reader expectation. Treating them as equal creates revision noise.

## Try this on the manuscript

Sort comments into observation, possible cause, suggested fix, and preference. Then look for comments that point to the same underlying pattern. Those clusters deserve attention before isolated line-level suggestions.

## What not to assume

Do not try to satisfy every comment. Your job is not to make every reader happy; it is to decide which evidence identifies a problem worth solving in this manuscript.

## What a professional read adds



### A useful boundary

This kind of self-check can tell you what to investigate. It cannot reproduce a sustained outside read, because the outside reader does not carry your private knowledge of the manuscript. That is where professional reader intelligence becomes useful: not as a louder opinion, but as a disciplined account of what the page made available to someone who did not write it.


**Professional lens:** Revision prioritization.  
**Related craft focus:** Feedback triage: pattern, leverage, priority, author decision.

**Next step:** If the manuscript still leaves the cause unclear after this test, that uncertainty is itself useful evidence for an outside diagnostic read.
