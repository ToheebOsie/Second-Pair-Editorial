# First-12 Content Cluster — Revised editorial drafts

## What is here

- `long-form/` — twelve topic-specific articles about recurring manuscript and feedback problems.
- `newsletters/` — twelve shorter notes, each written around the particular problem rather than a shared newsletter template.
- `social/120-derivatives.md` — 120 draft posts (ten different angles for each of the twelve problems). These are publishable starting drafts, not instructions to generate more copy.

## How the material was written

The supplied author/editor corpus is qualitative source material. It gives us real concerns and ordinary ways writers describe them: a slow first chapter, a character who makes sense to the author but not the reader, dialogue that sounds alike, a reader who understands the plot but does not care, too much description, confusing scenes, and feedback that points in opposite directions.

The content uses those concerns as starting points. It does not reproduce source conversations, invent testimonials or claim that the examples represent all authors.

## Editorial rules

- Answer the author's question before mentioning a service.
- Give each problem its own explanation and example. Do not reuse one article skeleton or repeat the same professional-reader paragraph across topics.
- Use plain language. Explain terms such as pacing, scene function or reader investment when they are useful.
- Do not turn preferences into universal rules. Genre, audience, form and author intention matter.
- Separate what a reader experienced from what they think caused it and from the change they recommend.
- Keep uncertainty honest. A short excerpt cannot establish a whole-manuscript problem.
- Do not write fake reader quotes, testimonials or claims of measured market prevalence.
- Mention a service only when it is a natural next step for the problem being discussed.

## Before publication

A human editor should check each piece against the live problem page, confirm links and review the platform's length and format. These files are editorial drafts, not proof that the articles or posts have been published.
