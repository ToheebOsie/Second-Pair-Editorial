# Q01 — Why does my opening feel slow?

**Canonical page:** `/problems/opening-feels-slow`  
**Tool:** /tools/reader-friction  
**Priority:** First inbound-authority cluster

## Reader problem
“Slow” can mean a few different things from the reader’s side: I may not have a question yet, I may not know what matters, or the story may be taking a while to change. The useful part is figuring out which experience you are actually seeing.

## Do not jump to the obvious fix
Cutting words may help, but we would not start there. If the reader is not curious yet, making the opening shorter can leave the same problem—just less of it.

## Five-question investigation
"What question does the opening make the reader want answered?","When does the protagonist’s immediate goal become legible?","Is information arriving before the reader has a reason to want it?","Do early scenes change the situation, or mainly explain it?","Does the same issue recur after the opening?"

## What professional investigation adds
We would look for the point where the reader’s attention changes, then go back to the page and ask what caused it. Sometimes it is information; sometimes it is stakes, scene function, expectation, or something less obvious.

## Professional lens
Reader attention, story promise, progression, scene function and the difference between word count and felt momentum.
