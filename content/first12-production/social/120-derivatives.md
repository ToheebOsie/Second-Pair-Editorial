| Why does my opening feel slow? | diagnostic question | Ask: what would a cold reader reasonably know, expect, or feel at the point where “why does my opening feel slow" becomes noticeable? |
| Why does my opening feel slow? | myth check | Myth: Do not cut the opening simply because a reader called it slow. Better question: what evidence would distinguish the competing explanations? |
| Why does my opening feel slow? | reader observation | Notice where the reading experience changes in a passage related to “Why does my opening feel slow?”. Describe the change before explaining it. |
| Why does my opening feel slow? | mini-case | A manuscript can show the symptom “why does my opening feel slow?” while the underlying mechanism is opening attention. Explain why the two are not identical. |
| Why does my opening feel slow? | before/after test | Before revision, state the intended reader change. After revision, reread for that effect rather than for whether the prose merely sounds cleaner. |
| Why does my opening feel slow? | contrarian observation | “Why does my opening feel slow?” does not automatically mean the most obvious fix. Do not cut the opening simply because a reader called it slow. First establish what they were waiting for. A quiet opening can work if the reader has a reason to keep looking forward. |
| Why does my opening feel slow? | author exercise | Write down the first concrete question you carried out of the opening. Then mark the next place where the manuscript gives that question more pressure, information, danger, desire, or consequence. If nothing develops it, you may have a handoff problem rather than a word-count problem. |
| Why does my opening feel slow? | professional distinction | Opening attention is a lens, not a law. Use it to connect reader experience to page evidence. |
| Why does my opening feel slow? | common misdiagnosis | Do not cut the opening simply because a reader called it slow. First establish what they were waiting for. A quiet opening can work if the reader has a reason to keep looking forward. |
| Why does my opening feel slow? | soft route to deeper investigation | If you can describe the symptom but cannot establish its cause from the manuscript alone, that is the point at which an outside diagnostic read can add value. |
