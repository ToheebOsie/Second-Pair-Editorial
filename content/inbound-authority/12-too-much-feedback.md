# Q12 — I have lots of feedback. How do I know what actually matters?

**Canonical page:** `/problems/too-much-feedback`  
**Tool:** /tools/feedback-conflict  
**Priority:** First inbound-authority cluster

## Reader problem
A long feedback file can leave you less certain than you were before. The useful part is working out which comments describe the same thing, which are personal preferences, and which point to a problem worth solving.

## Do not jump to the obvious fix
Ten people mentioning something does not automatically make it the biggest problem. One comment can sometimes point to a larger issue that explains several smaller ones.

## Five-question investigation
"Which comments describe the same underlying issue?","Which are isolated preferences?","Which concerns affect the manuscript at a larger scale?","Which changes would solve several smaller symptoms at once?","What should you test first?"

## What professional investigation adds
We would group the comments, separate what readers actually observed from what they think caused it, and then work out which question is worth testing first.

## Professional lens
Pattern recognition, evidence weighting, conflicting advice, revision priorities and preserving author decision-making.

## Content ladder
1. Problem page answers the question.
2. Newsletter gives one useful distinction and one manuscript test.
3. Long-form article shows how the symptom can have different causes.
4. Social content extracts one sharp observation.
5. Tool provides a self-check where appropriate.
6. Service appears only when the remaining question requires manuscript-level investigation.

## Conversion principle
Make the author more capable of judging the manuscript first. The premium service is the next step when the answer cannot be established reliably from a checklist alone.
