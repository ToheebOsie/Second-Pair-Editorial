# Second Pair Editorial — Client Intake and Service Routing

## First question

Ask:

**“What would you most like to understand or improve about the manuscript?”**

Then route from the answer.

## Routing map

### “How does the story land?”
Likely Beta Reading.

### “Is the developing story working?”
Likely Alpha Reading.

### “Something feels wrong, but I cannot find it.”
Likely Manuscript Diagnostic.

### “I know what needs to change and want help making the revision.”
Likely Developmental Editing.

### “I changed the manuscript and want another set of eyes.”
Likely Revision Review.

### “The story works; I want the prose cleaned up.”
Likely Line or Copy Editing.

### “I am at the final stage and want errors caught.”
Likely Proofreading.

### “I don't know.”
Do not force a service. Use the sample or a short conversation to establish the problem.

## Scope checks

Before quoting a larger project, confirm:

- word count
- manuscript stage
- requested service
- what the author wants answered
- whether the sample suggests a broader issue
- deliverables
- what is outside scope

## Decision rule

Choose the **smallest service that can reasonably answer the author's question**.

If the author needs a larger service, explain why in plain language and point to the evidence.

## Quote language

Separate:

- estimated scope
- confirmed scope
- confirmed price
- optional additions

Never imply that a sample review is a full manuscript diagnosis.
