# A reader should have questions. They should not have to guess what happened.

**Second Pair Newsletter**  
**Canonical problem:** `/problems/confusing-vs-productive-mystery`

A good mystery leaves something unanswered. Confusing writing can leave the reader unsure who entered the room, what the character saw or whether the conversation happened at all.

Those are not the same kind of uncertainty.

Make two lists for a scene: **what the reader should know** and **what you want the reader to wonder about**. If the reader is meant to wonder who sent the message, they should still know who received it. If they are meant to question a witness, they need to understand what the witness actually said.

You do not have to explain every clue. You do need to give the reader enough ground to recognise that a clue exists.

**Try this:** Ask someone to describe what happened in the scene before asking them what they think it means. If the event itself is unclear, the mystery may not be the problem.
