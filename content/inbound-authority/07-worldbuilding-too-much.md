# Q07 — How much worldbuilding is too much?

**Canonical page:** `/problems/worldbuilding-too-much`  
**Tool:** No dedicated tool yet  
**Priority:** First inbound-authority cluster

## Reader problem
There is no magic amount of worldbuilding that is “too much.” The better question is whether the information is doing something for me right now—orienting me, making me curious, changing the stakes, or affecting what I expect.

## Do not jump to the obvious fix
We would not automatically cut worldbuilding. Some of the richest parts of a story are there precisely because the world has weight. The issue is whether the information arrives before I have a reason to want it.

## Five-question investigation
"Does the reader need this information now?","Does it change what the reader understands or expects?","Can the information be carried by action rather than explanation?","Is the reader receiving several new concepts before they have anchors?","Does the same information recur unnecessarily?"

## What professional investigation adds
We would look at when the information arrives, what the reader can anchor it to, and what changes because they know it. That tells us much more than a word-count rule ever could.

## Professional lens
Selection, timing, relevance, system consistency and worldbuilding as story pressure rather than information inventory.
