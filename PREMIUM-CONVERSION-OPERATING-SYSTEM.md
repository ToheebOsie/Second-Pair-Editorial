# Second Pair — Premium Conversion & Operating System

## Purpose

Turn a qualified inbound inquiry into a calm, evidence-led premium buying experience without reverting to commodity editorial sales.

The operating principle is:

> Answer the author's question first. Recommend the service second. Price the agreed scope third.

## 1. Funnel

DISCOVERY → INQUIRY → REVIEW → CLARIFICATION → ROUTE → SCOPE → PROPOSAL → DECISION → BOOKING → HANDOFF → DELIVERY → FOLLOW-UP → REFERRAL / CASE STUDY

## 2. Inquiry triage SLA

Within one business day where capacity permits:

1. acknowledge receipt;
2. confirm whether the supplied material is sufficient for an initial assessment;
3. identify missing information only when it can change routing or scope;
4. assign a review status: READY / NEEDS-INFO / NOT-A-FIT / WAITLIST.

Do not promise a project date before capacity is checked.

## 3. Review record

Every qualified inquiry gets a private record containing:

- author concern in one sentence;
- manuscript stage;
- evidence supplied;
- previous feedback;
- revision history when relevant;
- desired decision/outcome;
- likely route;
- confidence in route;
- unresolved questions;
- scope assumptions;
- capacity status.

## 4. Route selection

Choose the smallest service that can reasonably answer the author's actual question.

If the author selected a service that does not fit, explain the mismatch in plain language and show what evidence led to the alternative.

## 5. Premium proposal structure

Every proposal should contain:

### A. What we understand
One short paragraph stating the author's concern and desired decision.

### B. What we recommend
Named route and why it answers that question.

### C. What we will examine
Specific manuscript dimensions, not vague promises of “deep feedback.”

### D. Deliverables
Report, annotations, revision plan, edited manuscript, or other agreed outputs.

### E. Scope boundaries
What is explicitly not included.

### F. Timing
Start window and delivery expectation only after capacity is confirmed.

### G. Investment
Price and pricing basis. For diagnostic/development work, use scope/complexity rather than automatically defaulting to a per-word commodity rate.

### H. Decision
A clear acceptance step and expiry/availability note only when genuinely necessary.

## 6. Capacity policy

Use four states:

- OPEN — normal acceptance;
- LIMITED — accept only projects matching current capacity;
- WAITLIST — offer a future start window;
- CLOSED — do not accept new work until capacity reopens.

Never manufacture scarcity.

## 7. Scope-change policy

If new information materially changes the work:

1. identify the change;
2. explain its effect on deliverables/time/cost;
3. obtain written approval before doing the additional work.

No silent scope creep.

## 8. Booking

Before work starts, confirm:

- exact manuscript version;
- service;
- deliverables;
- exclusions;
- price;
- payment status/terms;
- start date;
- delivery date;
- contact channel.

## 9. Handoff

The editor receives the approved scope plus the author's original question. The commercial record should never replace the manuscript evidence; it only supplies context.

## 10. Delivery

The delivery message should point to:

1. the main finding;
2. the top priorities;
3. the attached deliverables;
4. what the author should do first;
5. optional next steps.

## 11. Follow-up

Follow-up is decision support, not pressure.

Examples:

- Diagnostic → discuss priorities if the author requests help.
- Development → clarify the revision path.
- Revision Review → assess whether another revision or precision pass is justified.
- Line/Copy → identify whether proofreading is now appropriate.

Do not run generic “checking in” sequences merely to force another sale.

## 12. Repeat work

A returning client should not be forced through the entire discovery funnel again. Reuse the prior record while re-confirming the current manuscript version and current question.

## 13. Referral/case-study request

Ask only after useful work has been delivered and the client has had a reasonable opportunity to assess it. Request permission separately from feedback. Never condition service quality, discounts or future access on a testimonial.
